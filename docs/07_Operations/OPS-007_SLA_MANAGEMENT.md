# 1. Purpose

This document defines the Service Level Agreement (SLA) framework for the Digital Investment Platform.

Its objective is to establish measurable service targets, operational responsibilities and customer expectations.

---

# 2. SLA Principles

Service delivery shall follow these principles:

- Availability First
- Customer Transparency
- Measurable Performance
- Continuous Monitoring
- Accountability
- Continuous Improvement

SLA commitments should always be realistic and measurable.

---

# 3. Scope

SLA management applies to:

- Production Platform
- Customer Portal
- Administrative Portal
- Public APIs
- Authentication Services
- Payment Services
- Customer Support
- Incident Management

---

# 4. Objectives

The SLA framework aims to:

- Define service expectations.
- Improve customer satisfaction.
- Reduce operational uncertainty.
- Measure service quality.
- Support continual improvement.

---

# 5. Service Availability Targets

| Service | Target Availability |
|----------|---------------------|
| Customer Portal | 99.9% |
| Administrative Portal | 99.9% |
| Public APIs | 99.95% |
| Authentication Services | 99.95% |
| Payment Services | 99.95% |

Availability calculations exclude approved maintenance windows.

---

# 6. Incident Priority Levels

| Priority | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| P1 | Critical service outage |
| P2 | Major degradation |
| P3 | Moderate issue |
| P4 | Minor issue |

Incident priorities determine response and resolution objectives.

---

# 7. Response Time Targets

| Priority | Initial Response |
|----------|------------------|
| P1 | 15 Minutes |
| P2 | 30 Minutes |
| P3 | 2 Hours |
| P4 | 8 Business Hours |

Response time begins when the incident is officially recorded.

---

# 8. Resolution Time Targets

| Priority | Target Resolution |
|----------|-------------------|
| P1 | 4 Hours |
| P2 | 8 Hours |
| P3 | 2 Business Days |
| P4 | 5 Business Days |

Resolution targets should be reviewed periodically based on operational performance.

---

# 9. Escalation SLA

Escalation procedures ensure that unresolved issues receive timely attention from higher support levels.

Escalation should occur automatically according to predefined thresholds.

---

## Escalation Levels

| Level | Responsible Team |
|--------|------------------|
| Level 1 | Customer Support |
| Level 2 | Operations Team |
| Level 3 | DevOps / Infrastructure |
| Level 4 | Executive Management |

Escalation should occur before SLA targets are breached whenever possible.

---

# 10. Customer Communication

Customers should receive timely updates regarding incidents affecting service availability.

Communication should be:

- Accurate
- Transparent
- Timely
- Consistent

---

## Communication Events

Customers should be informed when:

- Major incidents occur.
- Planned maintenance begins.
- Planned maintenance ends.
- Service is restored.
- Significant SLA breaches occur.

Communication channels may include:

- Status Page
- Email
- Customer Portal
- SMS (Critical Events)

---

# 11. SLA Reporting

Operational performance should be measured and reported regularly.

Reports should support both operational improvement and executive oversight.

---

## Standard SLA Reports

Examples include:

- Monthly Availability Report
- Incident Performance Report
- Response Time Report
- Resolution Time Report
- Customer Support Report
- SLA Compliance Report

Reports should be retained according to governance policies.

---

## SLA Metrics Matrix

| Metric | Target | Measurement Method | Review Frequency |
|---------|---------|--------------------|------------------|
| Availability | ≥99.9% | Monitoring Platform | Monthly |
| Mean Time to Detect (MTTD) | <10 Minutes | Monitoring Logs | Monthly |
| Mean Time to Recover (MTTR) | <60 Minutes | Incident Reports | Monthly |
| First Response Time | By Priority | Ticketing System | Weekly |
| Resolution Time | By Priority | Ticketing System | Weekly |
| SLA Compliance | ≥98% | SLA Dashboard | Monthly |

Metrics should be reviewed during operational governance meetings.

---

# 12. SLA Breach Management

Any SLA breach should trigger formal review.

The review should identify:

- Root cause
- Operational impact
- Customer impact
- Corrective actions
- Preventive actions

Every breach should be documented.

---

## Corrective Actions

Following an SLA breach, teams should:

- Restore service
- Investigate root cause
- Update documentation
- Improve operational procedures
- Review monitoring rules

Continuous improvement should reduce future breaches.

---

# 13. Service Credits Policy

Where contractual obligations require compensation, service credits should follow documented commercial agreements.

Service credits should:

- Be calculated consistently.
- Require documented approval.
- Be traceable.
- Follow applicable contracts.

This policy does not replace contractual terms agreed with customers.

---

# 14. AI Implementation Notes

Artificial Intelligence may assist operational teams by analysing SLA performance, identifying trends and generating reports.

AI should never independently determine contractual compliance or customer compensation.

---

## Approved AI Responsibilities

AI may assist with:

- SLA reporting
- Trend analysis
- Performance forecasting
- Dashboard summaries
- Compliance reporting
- Operational recommendations

---

## Restricted AI Responsibilities

AI must not:

- Approve SLA exceptions.
- Calculate contractual compensation without human review.
- Modify SLA targets.
- Close customer incidents automatically.
- Override escalation procedures.

---

## Human Responsibility

Final responsibility for SLA compliance remains with the Operations Team, Customer Support Team, Executive Management and authorised business representatives.

All contractual decisions require human approval.
